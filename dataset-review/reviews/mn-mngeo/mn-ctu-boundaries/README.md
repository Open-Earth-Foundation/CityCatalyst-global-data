# Minnesota Geospatial Information Office (MnGeo / MnDOT) — City, Township, and Unorganized Territory boundaries

The authoritative boundary layer for Minnesota's minor civil divisions: every city, civil township, and unorganized territory (CTU) in the state as a polygon, with a GNIS feature id, name, CTU class, county, and population. MnGeo built the layer by updating MnDOT's municipal boundary file (maintained since 2014) against the Office of Administrative Hearings' boundary-adjustment records; it is refreshed weekly on the Minnesota Geospatial Commons. We review it as the **city-geometry and join spine for Minnesota** — the piece our emissions sources (RII, GreenStep) lack: a stable GNIS-coded, name-resolved, geometry-bearing list of MN cities that the name-only emissions tables can hang off, and the bridge from those tables to our canonical `locode` city key. This entry covers the **2026-07** retrieval of the shapefile distribution (dbf vintage 2026-04-09); because the Commons publishes a live weekly layer with no versioned editions, what we hold is a dated snapshot, not a frozen release.

## Canonical downloads

The dataset is published on the Minnesota Geospatial Commons as one package in four distributions — Shapefile, OGC GeoPackage, ESRI File Geodatabase, and a live ESRI FeatureServer — plus a full metadata record **verified**. The distributions carry the same features and attributes; the format choice is a convenience, not a content difference. This entry profiled the **Shapefile** distribution (`city_township_unorg.*`), retrieved 2026-07-05 and read in full. For a pipeline, prefer the **GeoPackage or FileGDB** over the shapefile: the shapefile's DBF truncates every field name to ten characters (`GNIS_FEATU`, `FEATURE_NA`, `CTU_CLASS`, `COUNTY_GNI`, `COUNTY_COD`, `COUNTY_NAM`), whereas the GeoPackage keeps the full names **verified**. The live FeatureServer is the option to poll if you want current boundaries without re-downloading.

## Why we use it

- It is the missing **geometry + stable identifier** layer for Minnesota cities: 856 cities as polygons, each with a GNIS feature id, county, and population. Our MN emissions sources key on city *name* alone, which is fragile; this layer supplies the coded, geometry-backed spine they can join to, and is the natural home for the eventual GNIS→`locode` crosswalk that ties MN city data into the modelled schema (need: `2026-07-mn-city-ghgi`, and reusable for any future MN city dataset).
- It pairs directly with **RII** and the **GreenStep Sustainability Index**: a name join lands effectively every RII city and ~98% of GreenStep communities against this layer (see Spatial and temporal scope for the exact figures), so it is the concrete instrument for validating and geolocating both. Use this layer as the authority, RII for the emissions values, GreenStep for which communities have an inventory.

## Spatial and temporal scope

Coverage is the whole state of Minnesota at the minor-civil-division level: 2,744 features — **906 city rows (856 distinct cities), 1,775 townships, and 62 unorganized territories** **verified**. Geometry is polygon, projected in **UTM zone 15N / NAD83 (EPSG:26915)**, not geographic — reproject to EPSG:4326 before any locode or web-map join **verified**. Boundaries are political (civil) divisions as recognised by the State, nominally 1:24,000 scale (±12 m), suitable for general reference and city-level attribution but not for survey-grade measurement.

The layer is **not frozen**: it is republished weekly and boundaries change as annexations and detachments are recorded, so re-downloading later returns adjusted polygons rather than this exact snapshot — pin the 2026-07 retrieval date when using it as a fixed reference **verified**. The `POPULATION` attribute is carried per feature; for a multi-county city it is the population of that county portion, so the city total is the sum across the city's rows (e.g. Lake City: 4,480 in Wabasha + 904 in Goodhue = 5,384) **verified**. The population vintage/source is not established from the shapefile itself and should be read from the full metadata record before the field is used analytically **unanswered**.

On the join to what we already hold (profiled 2026-07-05, results in `data/mn_ctu_cities_crosswalk_2026-07.csv` and reproducible from the raw): against **RII's 117 cities**, a normalised-name match finds all of them present in the layer — 116 as class CITY and one, White Bear Township, correctly as class TOWNSHIP, which is a real place RII tracks, not a miss **verified**. Against the **GreenStep Sustainability Index's 147 City-type communities**, 144 match; the three that do not — "Eden Prarie", "LaPrairie", "Virgina" — are **source-side spelling errors** in GreenStep (Eden Prairie, La Prairie, Virginia), not gaps in this layer **verified**. So this layer is a near-complete authority for both, and it doubles as a spell-check on the emissions sources.

## Interpretation warnings

The value of this layer is as an **authority and join key, not a data source about cities** — it tells you a city's boundary, GNIS id, county, and population, nothing about emissions or climate. Its risk is entirely in how it is joined, and three structural facts govern that. First, **a city that spans county lines is stored as one row per county, all sharing one GNIS feature id** (48 cities, e.g. Blaine across Anoka and Ramsey, Lake City across Wabasha and Goodhue). Any per-city operation must **dissolve or group by `GNIS_FEATU`** first; grouping by name or counting rows will over-count these cities and, as above, split their population **verified**.

Second, **name is not a key**. One name, "Saint Anthony", maps to two genuinely different cities with distinct GNIS ids (the Hennepin/Ramsey suburb and the Stearns County city); and the same string recurs across the three CTU classes (a "White Bear Lake" city and a "White Bear" township both exist). Join on `GNIS_FEATU`, and when coming from a name-only source, resolve to GNIS with county as the tiebreaker — never merge external data on name alone across the full file **verified**.

Third, **filtering to `CTU_CLASS = 'CITY'` will silently drop legitimately-tracked places**. RII includes White Bear Township, a township, not a city; a hard city-only filter loses it. Keep `CTU_CLASS` as an attribute and decide inclusion per use, rather than discarding non-city rows up front **verified**.

Finally, there is **no `locode` in this layer** — the repo's canonical city key. Relating this to the modelled schema needs a one-time GNIS→LOCODE crosswalk (the `locode` column in the committed crosswalk is present but empty, to be filled in that step); GNIS is the natural anchor because it is national, stable, and already carried here **verified**.

## Parsing notes

Read the GeoPackage if you can, to avoid the shapefile's **ten-character truncated field names** (`GNIS_FEATU` = GNIS feature id, `FEATURE_NA` = name, `COUNTY_GNI`/`COUNTY_COD`/`COUNTY_NAM` = county id/code/name); the GeoPackage and FileGDB carry the untruncated names **verified**. The shapefile's `.cpg` declares the encoding; names with diacritics or apostrophes should be read as UTF-8, not latin-1.

To go from an external name-keyed table to this layer, normalise both sides before matching: lower-case, strip periods, expand **"St." → "Saint" anywhere in the string** (not just leading — "North St. Paul", "West St. Paul", "Marine on St. Croix" all occur), and strip trailing role words like "Village" and "Township" before comparing, then match and re-attach the class. With that normaliser the RII and GreenStep matches above go through; without the mid-string "St." rule, half a dozen Saint-prefixed cities fall out spuriously. After matching, always dissolve/group by `GNIS_FEATU` (multi-county split, above) and carry `POPULATION` as a **sum**, not a first-value, for the multi-county cities.

The committed crosswalk `data/mn_ctu_cities_crosswalk_2026-07.csv` is the cities dissolved to one row per GNIS (856 rows: `gnis_feature_id, feature_name, ctu_class, n_county_parts, counties, population, locode`), built directly from the raw shapefile — the join-ready seed, with `locode` left blank for the crosswalk step. Alongside it, `data/mn_cities_2026-07.simplified.geojson` carries the same 856 cities **with geometry**, dissolved to one polygon per GNIS and **reprojected to EPSG:4326 (WGS84)** as GeoJSON requires; its properties mirror the crosswalk (`gnis_feature_id, feature_name, ctu_class, counties, population, locode`), so the two share a key. The committed GeoJSON is **geometry-simplified** (≈20 m, topology-preserving) to ~2 MB, in keeping with the source's own "general reference, not survey" positioning — use it for joins, mapping, and city-context; for full-resolution boundaries take the un-simplified `sample/mn_cities_2026-07.full.geojson` (~7 MB, gitignored) or regenerate from the raw distribution. The raw shapefile is re-downloadable from the Commons URLs above.
