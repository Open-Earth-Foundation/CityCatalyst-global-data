**METHODOLOGICAL NOTE**

**Municipal Feasibility Assessment for Climate Mitigation Actions**

**— Waste Sector —**

| This instrument assesses the feasibility of Chilean municipalities implementing climate mitigation actions in the waste sector. It analyses whether the current legal framework enables the municipality to act, whether it has the necessary institutional capacity, and whether accessible funding sources exist. Developed by SSG and Open Earth Foundation within the Municipal Mitigation Accelerator (MEED). It is expressed in two tabs: "Summary" with the integrated justification for each action, and "Assessment" with the breakdown by component, sub-criterion, and clickable regulatory references. |
| :---- |

**1\. Score Structure**

The total score (0–100) is calculated as a weighted sum of three components:

| Total Score \= Legal Feasibility × 40% \+ Local Governance × 30% \+ Financing × 30% |
| :---- |

Each component is obtained as a weighted sum of its sub-criteria, scored at three discrete levels:
100 — Fully meets the sub-criterion without relevant conditions.
50 — Partially meets, with conditions, gaps, or identified risks.

0 — Does not meet under the current regulatory and institutional context.

| Methodological note on weights (40/30/30): the choice is normative, not empirical. It reflects the priority assigned to legal risk as an enabling condition relative to governance and financing. For use in a specific municipality, the weights and values of the technical capacity sub-criterion should be recalibrated with local data, as this instrument operates as a national benchmark. |
| :---- |

| Range | Interpretation | Description |
| :---: | ----- | :---- |
| ≥ **75** | **Viable action** | Solid legal basis, sufficient institutional capacity, and accessible financing. Recommended for direct implementation. |
| **40–74** | **Viable with conditions** | Viable but requires overcoming identified barriers. Possible with a roadmap and prior strengthening. |
| **\< 40** | **Low current viability** | Significant barriers. Requires regulatory changes, institutional development, or extraordinary financing. |

**2\. Legal Feasibility (weight: 40%)**

Assesses whether the current regulatory framework authorises the municipality to implement the action. Three weighted sub-criteria:

| Legal \= Competence ownership × 60% \+ Legal restrictions × 30% \+ Alignment with national policy × 10% |
| :---- |

| Sub-criterion | Weight | 100 — Fully meets | 50 — Partially meets | 0 — Does not meet |
| :---- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |

| Ownership of competence — Can the municipality act directly? | 60% | A sectoral law (Law 18.695 or other) expressly assigns the function to the municipality. | The competence exists but is conditional, ambiguous, or mediated by an enabling instrument: tender, ordinance, Council agreement. | The competence belongs to another level of government. The municipality cannot act on its own. |
| :---- | :---: | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Legal restrictions — Absence of regulatory barriers** | **30%** | No restrictions. Does not require a ruling from the Comptroller General, SEIA, or any additional authorisation. | Moderate legal risk: may require prior authorisation, a process before the Comptroller, or precedents for administrative challenge exist. | A prohibition or express restriction exists, or a legal change would be required to act. |
| **Alignment with national policy — ENRO 2040, LMCC, MMA plans** | **10%** | The action is directly aligned with a current national goal or plan. | Alignment is partial or referential: thematic coherence without a direct mandate. | No alignment with current national policy, or a conflict with national regulations exists. |

| Regarding the "Legal restrictions" sub-criterion: in the absence of specific rulings from the Comptroller General validating or declaring certain designs illegal (e.g. PAYT with individualised measurement or extending ordinances beyond Law 21.100), the risk judgement is prudential, supported by the principle of strict legality and indirect case law. Each justification explicitly states when the risk is interpretive and has not been confirmed by a firm ruling. |
| :---- |

**3\. Local Governance (weight: 30%)**

Assesses the effective institutional capacity of the municipality to implement the action. Four weighted sub-criteria:

| Governance \= Technical capacity × 35% \+ Dependence on actors × 30% \+ Local instrument × 25% \+ Precedent × 10% |
| :---- |

| Sub-criterion | Weight | 100 — Fully meets | 50 — Partially meets | 0 — Does not meet |
| :---- | :---: | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Technical and institutional capacity** | **35%** | The municipality has its own technical team and documented experience in waste management. | Limited capacity. Requires external technical assistance or institutional strengthening to implement. | The vast majority of municipalities lack the technical capacity to implement this action autonomously. |
| **Dependence on external actors (inverse criterion)** | **30%** | The municipality can act autonomously. Coordination with external actors is low or manageable. | Requires significant coordination with actors outside its control: GORE, MMA, private REP entities, SEREMI. | Fragmented governance. The municipality cannot lead without key external actors. |
| **Enabling local instrument** | **25%** | A current local instrument exists (ordinance, PLADECO with environmental component, PACCC) that institutionalises the action. | The instrument is possible but requires drafting and approval by the Municipal Council. | No local instrument exists. The action would be entirely discretionary and vulnerable to changes in administration. |
| **Precedent in other municipalities** | **10%** | At least one Chilean municipality has a successful, evaluated, and replicable implementation. | Pilot experiences exist in Chile, without a consolidated model or evaluation of results. | No known precedent of implementation in Chile. |

| Note on municipal heterogeneity: the Waste and Beautification Unit (Art. 25, Law 18.695) is mandatory only in municipalities with more than 100,000 inhabitants; in smaller municipalities the function exists under Art. 3 d) but without a formal unit. This difference is reflected in governance justifications where analytically relevant, without modifying the numerical value of the sub-criterion, which operates as a national benchmark. |
| :---- |

**4\. Financing (weight: 30%)**

Assesses whether accessible funding sources exist to cover the cost of the action. Two weighted sub-criteria:

| Financing \= Availability of source × 75% \+ Accessibility of financing × 25% |
| :---- |

| Sub-criterion | Weight | 100 — Fully meets | 50 — Partially meets | 0 — Does not meet |
| :---- | :---: | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Availability of identified source** | **75%** | Explicit, accessible, and sufficient source to cover the real cost of the action. | Funds exist but are competitive, cover only partial components, or require prior BIP registration. | No specific accessible fund exists. The financial gap is significant. |
| **Accessibility of financing** | **25%** | The municipality can self-finance the action (DL 3.063 fees, fines) or a guaranteed direct transfer exists. | Competitive funds with a known process and reasonable probability of access. | Uncertain process, unattainable requirements, or an investment that exceeds the capacity of the typical municipality. |

| Reference sources: FNDR (environmental line), FPA MMA, DS 7/2017 MMA (Recycling Fund, Art. 31 REP Law 20.920), DS 312/2016 MINVU (Public Spaces Programme), PMU SUBDERE (Law 20.407, Programme 03 gloss 06), SERCOTEC (Law 19.207). |
| :---- |

**5\. Municipal Implementation Mechanisms**

Actions are implemented through one or more of the following mechanisms. The main mechanism is indicated in the Integrated Justification column of the Summary:

| Mechanism | Description | Examples |
| ----- | :---- | :---- |
| **Direct competence** | Express legal mandate. The municipality acts without depending on other actors. | icare\_0064 — Law 18.695 Art. 3 d) |
| **Tender specifications** | Municipal power exercised when contracting the service, imposing conditions on the operator. Without well-designed specifications, ownership is weakened. | c40\_0035, c40\_0037 — Law 18.695 Art. 6° |
| **Municipal ordinance** | Regulates locally and enforces. Can reinforce national standards. Cannot create new obligations without an express legal authorisation. | c40\_0040 (DL 3.063 Arts. 6°-9°) c40\_0034 (Law 21.100) |
| **Regional coordination** | The action exceeds the municipal scale. Requires GORE, SEREMI, or another actor. The municipality cannot act autonomously. | c40\_0036 (SEREMI Health, DS 189/2005) icare\_0122 (SEREMI MMA, DS 8/2012 Art. 5° d) |

**6\. Relevant Administrative and Judicial Case Law**

Rulings from the Comptroller General, Environmental Tribunal judgements, and doctrinal sources supporting the legal judgements in this instrument, with an indication of the state of available evidence:

| Type | Reference | Instrument action | Status |
| :---: | :---- | :---- | :---: |
| **CGR Ruling** | No. 29.860/2006 No. 9.294/2003 No. 30.585/2004 No. 33.416/2016 No. 4.059/2016 | c40\_0040 (PAYT): principle of strict legality in configuring the waste fee. Municipalities lack the authority to alter the tariff structure outside the parameters of DL 3.063 Arts. 7°-8°. | **Available** |
| **Administrative doctrine** | Exempt Resolution No. 9/2022 MMA — Municipal Ordinance Model REP (within the framework of DS 12/2020) | c40\_0037, icare\_0111: municipal ordinances are the enabled instrument for operationalising source separation, within the parameters of DS 12/2020. The municipal role is operational support, not regulatory. | **Available** |
| **Judgement** | Second Environmental Tribunal Case R-521-2025 (22/12/2025) Papier-Mettler Chile SpA / MMA (Law 21.100) | c40\_0034 (plastics): the MMA does not have the power of authentic interpretation of Law 21.100. The prohibition is national. Municipalities may enforce and reinforce, but not alter the core of the prohibition. | **Available** |
| **Gap** | No specific CGR ruling on individualised PAYT (volumetric measurement per household) | c40\_0040: the risk judgement of challenge is prudential, based on the principle of legality and the structure of DL 3.063. No firm ruling exists to date. | **Gap** |
| **Gap** | No judgement on the validity of municipal ordinances extending Law 21.100 or the REP Law beyond their parameters | c40\_0034, c40\_0037: risks supported by the principle of legality and the doctrine on municipal ordinances, not by firm case law on specific cases. | **Gap** |

| The identified gaps do not invalidate the instrument: they are grey areas explicitly recognised where legal risk is qualified as prudential. The instrument adopts a conservative stance: in the absence of a firm ruling, it applies the principle of strict legality. These gaps will only be resolved by future administrative or judicial case law. |
