# Regional Indicators Initiative (RII) — Minnesota city-scale indicators and GHG inventories

Annual, standardized city-wide metrics for participating Minnesota communities: energy (electricity, fossil gas, other heating fuels, district energy), potable water, on-road travel, and municipal solid waste, each with the associated greenhouse-gas emissions in CO2e and the retail cost. RII is produced by LHB, Inc. under ULI Minnesota, with data collection funded over time by the Minnesota Department of Commerce, the U.S. Department of Energy (EERE), MPCA, the McKnight Foundation, the Great Plains Institute, and the Metropolitan Council. This entry covers the live dataset as published through the RII site, read against the **February 2026** methodology; because the data is served as a live Tableau Public workbook with no versioned releases, what we hold is a dated snapshot rather than a frozen edition. The emissions values follow **ICLEI's U.S. Community Protocol**, not GPC — the practical consequences of that choice are the substance of this review.

## Why we use it

- It is the only single source that carries city-scale GHG emissions (tCO₂e) for many Minnesota cities at once — 117 cities for transport, 70 for waste, and 28 for energy — spanning Minneapolis, Saint Paul, Rochester, Duluth, and the inner/outer suburbs, which is what the CNB needs to seed a cohort city's context and what cohort selection needs to compare candidates. The breadth advantage no per-city inventory offers is real, but with the sharp caveat that coverage is per-indicator, not per-city: a full energy+transport+waste picture exists for only ~28 cities (need: `2026-07-mn-city-ghgi`).
- It pairs with the Minnesota Sustainability Index, which is a directory of *which* communities have an inventory and where, while RII holds the *numbers*: use the Index to find who has an inventory, use RII for the emissions themselves.
- It is annual and methodologically consistent across cities and years, so it supports a baseline-and-trajectory reading (RII's own stated purpose) rather than a single snapshot.

## Spatial and temporal scope

Geography is individual Minnesota cities, defined by municipal boundary, for the subset of communities RII has been funded to cover — self-selected and funding-driven, not a complete enumeration of the state. The values are local truth in the sense that they are built bottom-up from each city's own utility, travel, and waste data, not a modelled national prior; but coverage is uneven by design, because different funders prioritised different indicators and some cities self-funded, so a given city may carry one indicator but not the full set **verified**.

Profiling the exported data (2026-07-05) confirms this unevenness is severe, and it is the single most important thing to know before using RII: **each indicator covers a different set of cities** **verified**. After dropping the shared non-city "Ramsey County" row, transport covers 117 cities and includes all fifteen of the need's target cities, including Mankato and Duluth; waste covers 70 cities and includes thirteen targets but **not Duluth or Mankato**; and energy covers only 28 cities and includes only three targets (Rochester, Minnetonka, Saint Louis Park). There is no single "RII city" universe — a city with a transport figure very often has no energy or waste figure. Any join across indicators is an inner join on a shrinking set; all 28 energy cities happen to also carry transport and waste, so the full energy+transport+waste set is exactly those 28 cities and no more.

The 28-city energy slice is almost certainly a **filtered partial view, not RII's true energy coverage** **verified**. It is a geographic cluster of northeast-metro suburbs (Arden Hills, Blaine, the New Brighton / Shoreview / White Bear group, Roseville) plus a few outstate cities (Crookston, Northfield, Thief River Falls, Rochester); Minneapolis, Saint Paul, Bloomington, Duluth, and Edina are all absent, which is implausible for the real energy dataset. Treat this energy export as a scoped selection that was active in the Tableau viz at download time, and re-export the unfiltered energy view before drawing any coverage conclusion for energy.

Coverage is annual, beginning with three communities in 2010 and expanding since. All four exports are the **2024** data year: the transport export carries the year label on its sheet, and the analyst confirmed the energy and waste exports were taken from the same 2024 selection even though Tableau omitted the label on those sheets **verified**.

The exported values are **GHG emissions in tonnes CO₂e**, broken down by fuel (energy: electricity / natural gas / other) and by waste-management method (waste: incinerated / landfilled / recycled), with transport as a single on-road total **verified**. This was confirmed against three independent checks in the notebook, not assumed from the view names — the transport file in particular is named `vmt` but holds emissions, not vehicle-miles. The only genuine gaps are the water view (not exported; minor for GHG) and the unfiltered energy coverage. So city GHG totals for the covered cities are directly available from this export; no re-derivation from activity data is needed.

The data is not frozen. RII deliberately restates prior years when upstream sources revise their data or when its own methods improve, applying changes consistently across all cities and years, so re-downloading later returns revised numbers rather than this exact snapshot **verified**. Recent years are provisional: to publish on time, RII carries the previous year's value as a placeholder for several inputs (utility emissions factors, fuel economy, some fuel use) until the study-year data arrives, historically a sub-5% effect on totals but larger when electricity emissions factors move sharply **verified**.

## Interpretation warnings

The values are not GPC inventories, and this is the load-bearing caveat for us. RII follows ICLEI's U.S. Community Protocol; RII's own methodology explicitly contrasts itself with the GPC-aligned Metro Climate Stats and states the two are "not directly comparable or interchangeable" **verified**. Energy and transport translate to GPC BASIC sectors cleanly enough (see Parsing notes on the mapping), but waste does not: RII books the *future* methane from waste landfilled during the inventory year (the methane-commitment method) and places waste-to-energy in the waste sector, whereas GPC books emissions occurring *during* the inventory year (first-order-of-decay) and places waste-to-energy in energy. An RII waste figure and a GPC waste figure for the same city measure different things — treat RII waste as non-GPC-conformant until reconciled, not as a drop-in.

Do not compare RII numbers to any non-RII inventory. The methodology warns against mixing programs even within one city: using one program for a baseline year and another to track progress is explicitly called out as invalid, as is comparing an RII city to a city tracked only in Metro Climate Stats or the MPCA statewide inventory **verified**. For our use this means RII is internally consistent for cross-city and cross-year comparison *within RII*, but a city's RII value must not be blended with that city's own separately published CAP inventory.

Travel uses the in-boundary (geographic) method, not origin-destination. RII counts all on-road VMT occurring inside the city boundary (from MnDOT), so a city with heavy pass-through traffic reads *high* and a bedroom community with long outbound commutes reads *low*, relative to an origin-destination method like Metro Climate Stats' **verified**. This is a defensible territorial choice and is GPC-compatible as a scope-1 induced-activity alternative, but it changes which cities look like transport hotspots — do not read RII transport as commuter-attributable emissions.

Energy emissions are available split by sector (residential vs. commercial/industrial) and by fuel (electricity, natural gas, "other"), each in tCO₂e — the export delivers two files, one per sector, so a residential/commercial breakdown *is* recoverable for the cities present, which is better than the headline-only figure the methodology narrative implies **verified**. Electricity is Scope 2 (consumption-based, using utility-specific or regional-average factors) **verified**. One silent effect: utility data-privacy rules sometimes exclude very large customers to avoid revealing an individual user's consumption, which can *undercount* a city's energy and its emissions where a big industrial or institutional load is suppressed **verified**. For a GPC mapping, this res/com/fuel granularity maps usefully onto GPC's I.1 residential and I.2 commercial stationary-energy sub-sectors — but only for the 28 cities in the energy view, and note that RII's "commercial" bucket also carries industrial and street-lighting load, so it is not a clean GPC I.2.

Emissions use AR4 100-year GWPs (methane 25, nitrous oxide 298), chosen for consistency with U.S. EPA reporting, not the newer AR6 values **verified**. Any cross-walk to a dataset built on AR5/AR6 GWPs carries a small but real methane-driven discrepancy. Sources contributing collectively under 5% of a city's total are excluded as de minimis, and land-use / land-cover emissions are not accounted for at all **verified**. Non-road transport (rail, marine, air), off-road equipment, and agriculture are largely excluded or de-minimis — RII is deliberately scoped to the five activities a city government can influence, not a comprehensive territorial inventory **verified**.

## Parsing notes

Each RII indicator exports as its own Tableau crosstab `.xlsx`, one sheet ("Sheet 1"), wide, with the same recurring quirks **verified**. The first row is not a header in the pandas sense: the city name sits in column 0 with an empty top-left cell, and the category labels (fuel types, waste methods) sit in row 0 across the value columns — so a naive `read_excel` mislabels everything. There is a redundant measure-label column (column 1 repeats "Total Energy" / "Waste By Type" on every row) that carries no information and should be dropped. Read each file with `header=None`, then take column 0 as `city` and the row-0 labels as the value-column names.

The transport file (`vmt.xlsx`) has a *two-row* header, not one: row 0 is `[blank, "Year"]` and row 1 is `[blank, "2024"]`, with cities starting at row 2 — different from the energy and waste files, whose year is absent entirely. Any loader must special-case this extra header row and attach the year `2024`; the energy and waste sheets carry no year label but are the same 2024 selection (analyst-confirmed). Note the filename: it says `vmt`, but the values are transport *emissions* in tCO₂e, not vehicle-miles — a source misnomer that will mislead anyone who trusts the name.

Two content traps. The waste "Recycled" column is present but **all zeros** across every city — a placeholder column, not real data (recycling carries no emissions under the U.S. Community Protocol); do not read it as "this city recycles nothing." In fact this all-zero column is what proves the values are emissions rather than tonnage. And every file carries a non-city "Ramsey County" row mixed in with the municipalities — filter administrative types before any city-level statistic, exactly as with the Sustainability Index (it drops one row from each indicator, giving 28 / 70 / 117 cities).

Every value is **tonnes CO₂e**, not the activity units (MMBtu, VMT, short tons) that the methodology narrative describes — the exports are the emissions views. This is not obvious from the files and must not be assumed: the notebook confirms it three ways (waste recycled is zero by protocol; residential electricity read as tCO₂e implies a sane per-household kWh; transport reads at 2–4 tCO₂e/capita — all absurd under an activity-unit reading). Values arrive as clean numerics with no unit suffixes, so the unit label must be attached deliberately. RII also publishes each indicator under several normalizations (absolute, per capita, per job, per household); these exports are the absolute totals, but any future export must record which normalization it is, or per-capita and absolute values will be silently conflated **verified**. The cleaned tidy table is `releases/2026-07/data/rii_emissions_long.csv` (city × indicator × sector × subcategory × tCO₂e), built and validated by `rii_extract_clean.ipynb`.

For the GPC mapping, the target correspondence from RII's U.S. Community Protocol activities is: energy emissions, split residential vs. commercial/industrial and by fuel (electricity / natural gas / other) → GPC **I Stationary Energy** (scopes 1 and 2; the residential/commercial split maps usefully onto I.1/I.2, though RII's "commercial" also carries industrial and street-lighting load); on-road transport → GPC **II.1 Transportation** (in-boundary/territorial method); waste, split by management method → GPC **III Waste** (methodologically distinct — the methane-commitment basis and in-waste-sector WTE mean this is *flag, do not equate*). Water and cost are RII indicators with no direct GPC emissions home and stay out of the GHG mapping. Energy and transport are a tractable mapping; waste is the one genuinely lossy sector. The full unit-level mapping is built and validated in the release notebook → `data/rii_to_gpc_mapping.csv`.
