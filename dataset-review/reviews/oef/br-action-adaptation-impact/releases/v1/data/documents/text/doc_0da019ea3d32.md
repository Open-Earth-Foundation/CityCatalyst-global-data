Recommendations
     Establish sustainable food systems that include low-carbon agricultural practices to reduce
 1   greenhouse gases emissions and other environmental degradation.


     Brazil’s GHG emissions related to production of agricultural products is higher than those for
     consumption (either locally produced or imported). This disparity is associated with Brazil’s elevated
     agricultural exports. While these are major contributors to Brazilian economy, the local damage
     to the environment and people’s health is substantial. While the environmental impact occurs in
     Brazil, the consumption of local produce (and the driver of such impact) takes place elsewhere.
     Agricultural food production systems that assess and minimise agricultural GHG emissions are
     fundamental to building future solutions for sustainable and healthy food systems and a sustainable
     economy in Brazil.


     Implementing strategies, based on the Brazilian Food Guidelines, to promote healthy eating
 2   habits, food security, climate justice, and social equity.


     Suboptimal diets are a major health risk factor for the Brazilian population, with thousands of
     deaths associated with overconsumption of red and processed meat, the imbalanced intake of
     processed grains, as well as the lack of intake of healthy vegetables. This is despite the publication
     of the 2014 Brazilian Food Guide, which is an internationally respected and model dietary guideline
     for the world.11,12 Thus, it is essential to develop effective strategies to implement and amplify the
     transformative potential of the Food Guide for the Brazilian population to promote climate and
     health action from farm to fork, including promoting the guide through key health actors such as
     community health workers.
